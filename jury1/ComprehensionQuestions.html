NextGen Corporation is a developer of cellular phone communication technology. In 2024, NextGen received industry-level recognition as the Standard Essential Patent (SEP) holder for 6G (6th generation wireless technology) standard.

Adopting the same standard ensures compatibility across different devices. For NextGen, holding the SEP means that most manufacturers of future mobile devices such as smartphones will pay NextGen royalties to adopt its 6G standard. Recognition as the SEP holder, however, also requires that NextGen charge manufacturers adopting its technology a "fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory" (FRAND) royalty rate to ensure that manufacturers have access to the standard without facing excessive pricing or unfair restrictions.

PhoneMaker's latest phones sold on the market by using NextGen's 6G technology. During licensing negotiations, NextGen demanded a FRAND rate of $15 per cell phone sold. PhoneMaker rejected this rate, and filed a lawsuit against NextGen in a U.S. district court claiming that the $15 per device was breaching the FRAND terms and argued that $1.5 per device is a reasonable rate. In response, NextGen filed a lawsuit in the same court, accusing PhoneMaker of patent infringement and seeking damages from PhoneMaker.
