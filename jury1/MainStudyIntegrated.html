RECAP

PhoneMaker's latest phones use NextGen's standard essential patent - 6G technology. During negotiations, PhoneMaker and NextGen failed to agree on the "fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory" (FRAND) rate: NextGen requested $15 per phone and PhoneMaker proposed paying NextGen $1.5 per phone sold. They sued each other.



COURT ORDER

You have been selected as a juror to help the United States court determine the FRAND rate. FRAND terms require the royalty to be fair and reasonable to both the patent holder (NextGen) and the licensee (PhoneMaker).

Please carefully review the jury instructions below, and apply them to evaluate the evidence presented by both sides.

When deciding on the FRAND rate, consider the following legally relevant factors:

  • F1 What the patent owner has charged others for using the same patent in similar situations.
  • F2 The rates paid by the patent user for the use of other patents comparable to the patent in suit.
  • F3 Whether using the patent also helps sell other related products, either for the user or the patent owner.
  • F4 How the company actually used the patent and how much value it added—again, don't count extra value just because it's in a standard.
  • F5 What royalty rates are typical in this industry for similar inventions.
  • F6 How much of the product's success comes from the patented part, versus other things like marketing, design, or added features.
  • F7 What experts say is a fair royalty rate in this case.
  • F8 What royalty a reasonable buyer and seller would have agreed on when the use started, assuming both were negotiating fairly and honestly. This includes the value to the user and any other licenses they might have needed.
